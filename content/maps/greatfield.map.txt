# The Great Field - room for two armies of forty thousand.
#
# 72 x 96 cells at 25 m each = 1800 m x 2400 m. Built for the largest order of
# battle this project has: twenty regiments a side at two thousand men each,
# which at realistic frontages needs about two kilometres of line. Waterloo put
# seventy thousand on four kilometres, so this is not a generous field - it is
# a historically ordinary one for the number of men standing on it.
#
# Open plains between the deployment zones on purpose. The terrain that matters
# is at the edges and in the middle, and the ground either army forms up on is
# clear, so that what a regiment costs to move is about the other regiments
# rather than about the going underfoot.
#
#   - A range of small mountains across the north, anchoring that flank. There
#     is no way round it: an army that wants the northern end of the line has
#     to fight for it rather than march about it.
#   - Two knolls in no-man's land, one north of centre and one south. Both are
#     a real march from either start line, which is what makes taking them a
#     decision instead of a formality.
#   - The river along the southern edge, three cells (75 m) of it, anchoring
#     the other flank. Everything can wade - slowly, in disorder, and at a
#     defensive penalty - so the flank is anchored rather than sealed.
#   - One ford, where the road crosses at column 31. The only place a formed
#     body gets over without coming out the far side as a crowd.
#
# Deployment corridors are columns 4-18 in the west and 53-67 in the east,
# both left clear of anything but plains, with about a kilometre of open
# ground between the two front lines.

name     = The Great Field
cellSize = 25
